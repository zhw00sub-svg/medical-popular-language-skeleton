# Medical Popular Language Skeleton

Use this skill to make Chinese medical content understandable without making it casual, simplistic, or medically unsafe. Treat “人话” as **complete, natural, reader-oriented Chinese**, not colloquial speech and not shorter sentences.

Use it as the preferred language layer whenever the task substantially involves Chinese prose for science communication, patient explanation, a public lecture, or an academic lecture script. Pair it with an evidence, guideline, or clinical-review skill when facts are not yet locked; those skills decide what is medically supportable, while this skill decides how the reader can genuinely understand it.

## Preserve the Medical Contract

Before rewriting, extract or confirm:

- the fact, data, or recommendation being explained;
- the intended reader and what they are likely to misunderstand;
- the population, time point, and condition to which the statement applies;
- the strength of the evidence and uncertainty;
- red flags, contraindications, and the appropriate next step.

Do not invent mechanisms, numbers, diagnoses, treatment choices, drug advice, or reassurance. Do not turn an association into a cause, group evidence into an individual prediction, or general education into a diagnosis.

If a missing fact would change safety advice, state the gap and ask for it. Otherwise, write for an ordinary adult reader and label that assumption in the handoff.

## The Core Unit: Complete Expression

For every key medical judgment, make the reader able to answer these five questions without rereading:

1. **Who or what** is being discussed?
2. **What has happened or may happen?**
3. **Why does it happen, or what relationship has been observed?**
4. **Why does it matter to this reader?**
5. **What can and cannot be concluded or done now?**

Not every sentence needs all five parts. A paragraph must collectively carry the parts that matter. Keep natural Chinese ellipsis when the subject is genuinely obvious; restore it when ellipsis conceals cause, premise, responsibility, or action.

Prefer concrete verbs and named relationships:

```text
Weak: 术后存在营养风险，需要综合管理。
Complete: 手术后进食量和食物耐受会改变；如果蛋白质、维生素和微量元素的摄入没有随之调整，部分患者会出现相应缺乏。因此，复查和补充方案要按照手术方式、化验结果和进食情况一起决定，不能只凭感觉自行增减。
```

The rewrite is not required to be longer. Expand only the missing relationship; delete ceremony, repetition, and abstract padding.

## Writing Workflow

1. **Choose the reader task.** Decide whether the reader needs to understand a concept, interpret a risk, recognize a warning sign, weigh a treatment, prepare for a procedure, or carry out a daily action.
2. **Build the paragraph skeleton.** Select the closest pattern from `references/skeleton-library.md`. Adapt it; do not force every slot into every paragraph.
3. **Write from experience to explanation.** Begin with what readers may notice or ask, then explain the medical relationship, then give the correct interpretation and boundary.
4. **Make evidence visible when needed.** For numbers, name the population, outcome, time scale, and comparator before explaining meaning. For uncertainty, say what remains unknown instead of using empty hedging.
5. **Name the next action.** When action is appropriate, state who should act, what should trigger action, and where the action belongs: self-care, scheduled follow-up, clinician discussion, or urgent evaluation.
6. **Run the two final tests.** Complete the tests below before delivering.

## Tone and Sentence Rules

- Write as a calm, informed physician or medical writer speaking to an intelligent adult; do not imitate social-media speech, a classroom outline, or an academic abstract.
- Put one main relationship in each sentence. Let sentence length vary, but do not use fragments as a substitute for clarity.
- Introduce a technical term when it adds precision. Explain it once in ordinary language, then use it consistently.
- Explain a negation positively: after saying what a finding does not prove, state what it does support.
- Use metaphors only to illuminate a real relationship. Do not let a metaphor become the mechanism or treatment recommendation.
- Treat fear with precision: neither dramatize possible harm nor use warmth to erase red flags.

Avoid abstract endings such as “需要重视”“应综合评估”“属于系统性问题”“应规范管理” unless the text identifies what to observe, who evaluates it, and what decision that evaluation informs.

## The Two Final Tests

### 1. 顺读复述测试

Read the paragraph once as a non-specialist. Without looking back, could that reader restate:

- the medical fact or relationship;
- its relevance to them;
- the condition or boundary; and
- the appropriate next step, if one exists?

If not, restore the missing subject, object, premise, causal link, or role.

### 2. Medical Boundary Test

Confirm that the prose has not:

- upgraded possibility to certainty, association to causality, or risk to diagnosis;
- implied that a reader should start, stop, or change treatment without an appropriate clinical decision;
- hidden a red flag behind a reassuring transition;
- replaced population-level data with a promise about one person.

## Delivery Format

For a short passage, return the rewritten text and one concise note on the main misunderstanding corrected.

For a chapter, article, or lecture script, return:

- the revised text;
- the intended reader and any assumption made;
- the recurring skeletons used;
- a short list of places requiring evidence or author confirmation; and
- confirmation that no medical claim or action advice was added.

Read `references/skeleton-library.md` before drafting a new medical-popular text, reorganizing a section, or revising a high-stakes symptom, procedure, or treatment explanation.
